    How scoring works
    • Window: each forecast is saved at 6am Pacific and covers 6am to 6am the next day. Observations come from the nearest National Weather Service station (Napa County Airport for Napa; the nearest reporting station for Vallejo, which has none of its own).
    • High / low miss: forecast highest and lowest hourly temperature in the window minus the observed highest and lowest.
    • Hourly miss: the average gap between the forecast and observed temperature, hour by hour, across the 24 hours.
    • Rain: a source “calls rain” when any hour shows rain, storms or snow, or a 50%+ chance. Observed rain is any station report of rain, drizzle, showers or storms. Hit = called and it rained; Miss = it rained without a call; False alarm = called but stayed dry.
    • Rain timing: for hits, forecast start hour minus actual start. “+2h” means the forecast had it starting 2 hours later than it did.
    • Rain skill (Brier): compares the highest rain chance with what happened. 0 is perfect, lower is better.
    • Score (0–100): 70% temperature (100 minus 10 points per degree of average miss across high, low and hourly) and 30% rain (100 × (1 − Brier), or 100/0 for a right/wrong call when a source gives no rain chances). Just a handy single number for ranking.
    • Seasons: meteorological (Winter Dec–Feb, Spring Mar–May, Summer Jun–Aug, Fall Sep–Nov) and Bay Area wet (Nov–Apr) vs. dry (May–Oct).
    Data: National Weather Service (api.weather.gov), Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com, CC BY 4.0), MET Norway (api.met.no, CC BY 4.0), Pirate Weather (pirateweather.net), Tomorrow.io, WeatherAPI.com, Visual Crossing, OpenWeatherMap. Place search by Open-Meteo Geocoding.
