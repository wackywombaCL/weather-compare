    How scoring works
    • Window: each forecast is saved at 6am Pacific and covers 6am to 6am the next day. Observations: Napa uses Napa County Airport. Vallejo has no official station, so its temperatures, wind and humidity are the average of four nearby stations (north Vallejo, Hercules, Benicia and Cordelia; any one more than 4° off the others is dropped that hour). None of those measure rain, so whether and when it rained comes from Napa County Airport, the closest rain gauge, and the rain total is a blend of the Napa and Concord airport gauges, weighted toward Napa because it is closer.
    • High / low miss: forecast highest and lowest hourly temperature in the window minus the observed highest and lowest.
    • Hourly miss: the average gap between the forecast and observed temperature, hour by hour, across the 24 hours.
    • Rain: a source “calls rain” when any hour shows rain, storms or snow, or a 50%+ chance. Observed rain is any station report of rain, drizzle, showers or storms. Hit = called and it rained; Miss = it rained without a call; False alarm = called but stayed dry.
    • Rain timing: for hits, forecast start hour minus actual start. “+2h” means the forecast had it starting 2 hours later than it did.
    • Rain total: each source's expected rain for the window, compared with the measured total (in brackets: how far off). It counts toward the score on days rain was in play.
    • Rain skill (Brier): compares the highest rain chance with what happened. 0 is perfect, lower is better.
    • Score (0–100): 70% temperature (100 minus 10 points per degree of average miss across high, low and hourly) and 30% rain. The rain part is the rain call (100 × (1 − Brier), or 100/0 for a right/wrong call when a source gives no rain chances). On days when rain was in play (it rained, or the source expected some), 40% of the rain part is how close the rain total was: the miss compared with the larger of the forecast and measured totals. Just a handy single number for ranking.
    • “Usually within” on the Best card: the miss the Best forecast's highs stayed inside on 3 days out of 4, over its last 14 scored days at that range. It appears once the corrected Best forecast has 3 scored days; until then the card shows how closely the sources agree.
    • Smart average: a blend where each source's weight depends on its score over the previous 14 scored days (the best gets full weight; every 8 points behind it cuts the weight by about two-thirds). It needs 3+ scored days per source, and until then equals the plain average.
    • Recent accuracy shading (Forecast tab): each source's average score over its last 7 scored days. Green 88+ (Great), yellow 80+ (Good), orange 70+ (Fair), red under 70 (Poor).
    • Best forecast (top of the Forecast tab): each part of the forecast is blended from the sources that have been most accurate at that part over the last 14 scored days: high, low, hourly temperatures, rain chance (Brier), rain start time, rain amount and wind. The leader counts most; others fade out the further behind they are. Days 2+ use each source's accuracy at that range (1 and 2 days ahead). It's also scored here as its own row.
    • Warm/cold correction: before blending, each source's temperatures are corrected for how warm or cold it has run in that town over the last 14 scored days, hour by hour and for the high and low (for example, every source has been calling Vallejo's afternoons several degrees too cool). Only the Best forecast is corrected; each source's own numbers are shown as it gives them.
    • Rain memory: rain chance, timing and amount weights come from the last 10 days when rain was in play (it rained or a source called for it), not just the last 14 days, so a dry spell doesn't leave one wet day deciding who leads.
    • Right now: for Vallejo and Napa, the "now" temperature is what the stations are reading, and the next few hours of the Best forecast are nudged toward it (the nudge fades by about two-thirds every 3 hours). The 6am locked-in forecast gets the same nudge from the 6am reading.
    • Seasons: meteorological (Winter Dec–Feb, Spring Mar–May, Summer Jun–Aug, Fall Sep–Nov) and Bay Area wet (Nov–Apr) vs. dry (May–Oct).
