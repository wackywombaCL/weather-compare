    How scoring works
    • Window: each forecast is saved at 6am Pacific and covers 6am to 6am the next day. Observations: Napa uses Napa County Airport. Vallejo has no official station, so its temperatures, wind and humidity are the average of three nearby bay-side stations (Hercules, Benicia and Cordelia; any one more than 4° off the others is dropped that hour), and its rain comes from Napa County Airport, the closest station that measures rain.
    • High / low miss: forecast highest and lowest hourly temperature in the window minus the observed highest and lowest.
    • Hourly miss: the average gap between the forecast and observed temperature, hour by hour, across the 24 hours.
    • Rain: a source “calls rain” when any hour shows rain, storms or snow, or a 50%+ chance. Observed rain is any station report of rain, drizzle, showers or storms. Hit = called and it rained; Miss = it rained without a call; False alarm = called but stayed dry.
    • Rain timing: for hits, forecast start hour minus actual start. “+2h” means the forecast had it starting 2 hours later than it did.
    • Rain total: each source's expected rain for the window, compared with the station's measured total (in brackets: how far off). Not part of the score yet; it's shown so you can see who gets amounts right.
    • Rain skill (Brier): compares the highest rain chance with what happened. 0 is perfect, lower is better.
    • Score (0–100): 70% temperature (100 minus 10 points per degree of average miss across high, low and hourly) and 30% rain (100 × (1 − Brier), or 100/0 for a right/wrong call when a source gives no rain chances). Just a handy single number for ranking.
    • Smart average: a blend where each source's weight depends on its score over the previous 14 scored days (the best gets full weight; every 8 points behind it cuts the weight by about two-thirds). It needs 3+ scored days per source, and until then equals the plain average.
    • Recent accuracy shading (Forecast tab): each source's average score over its last 7 scored days. Green 88+ (Great), yellow 80+ (Good), orange 70+ (Fair), red under 70 (Poor).
    • Best forecast (top of the Forecast tab): each part of the forecast is blended from the sources that have been most accurate at that part over the last 14 scored days: high, low, hourly temperatures, rain chance (Brier), rain start time, rain amount and wind. The leader counts most; others fade out the further behind they are. Days 2+ use each source's accuracy at that range (1 and 2 days ahead). It's also scored here as its own row.
    • Seasons: meteorological (Winter Dec–Feb, Spring Mar–May, Summer Jun–Aug, Fall Sep–Nov) and Bay Area wet (Nov–Apr) vs. dry (May–Oct).
